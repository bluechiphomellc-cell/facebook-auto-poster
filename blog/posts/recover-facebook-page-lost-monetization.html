How to Recover a Facebook Page That Lost Monetization

August 13, 2026 · 8 min read

Your page just lost monetization. You log into Meta Business Suite, navigate to the monetization section, and see the dreaded "not eligible" banner. Your in-stream ads stop earning. Your performance bonuses pause. Income drops to zero overnight.

This post is what to actually do in that situation. Not the official Meta documentation. The real recovery playbook based on what works and what does not.

What "lost monetization" actually means

It is not one state. Facebook has several distinct monetization-loss states:

Temporary pause. You dropped below the 60k-minute video threshold or got hit with a single policy strike. Usually recovers in 30-60 days if you fix the underlying issue.

Restricted eligibility. Multiple policy strikes or sustained low-quality signals. Recovery takes 60-90 days minimum and requires demonstrable behavior change.

Indefinite suspension. Pattern of violations or one severe violation (copyrighted content uploaded at scale, repeated misinformation flags, paid engagement detected). Recovery is possible but rare and slow.

Permanent ban. Severe single violation (CSAM, incitement, mass spam, large-scale ToS abuse) or accumulated violations after restoration. Recovery is essentially impossible.

The first step in any recovery is identifying which state you are in. The recovery path is completely different for each.

Step 1: Figure out the specific reason

Meta Business Suite tells you exactly why eligibility was pulled. Look under Monetization, then Eligibility. The message names the specific policy or threshold.

Common reasons:

Unoriginal content. Your videos look like they came from other sources without sufficient transformation. By far the most common reason in 2026. Facebook tightened this enforcement substantially.

Engagement bait. Captions like "tag a friend" or "comment if you agree" got flagged. Quick to fix on individual posts but hard to clean up retroactively.

Misinformation or borderline content. Posts that got reported repeatedly or fact-checked counted against the page.

Inconsistent posting. Page went dark for weeks. Video minutes dropped below 60k in the 60-day window. Eligibility pulled automatically.

Copyrighted music. Reels with licensed-but-not-licensed-to-you audio. Auto-detected by Meta's content match.

Repeated policy violations. Several individual posts got removed or demoted. Threshold crossed.

Each of these has a different fix path. Our content monetization eligibility guide covers the underlying rules in detail.

Step 2: Understand the cooldown

Meta has a cooldown period after eligibility loss. You cannot rush this.

For most policy violations the cooldown is 30 days minimum. For unoriginal content violations the cooldown is typically 60 days. For repeat violations the cooldown can stretch to 90 days or more.

During the cooldown, your page still posts and accumulates engagement. But your monetization eligibility status will not be re-evaluated. There is no point appealing during the cooldown window. Meta will respond with "your page is in review" and nothing changes.

The frustrating part is that the cooldown is invisible. You will not see a clock ticking down. You just have to wait and check periodically.

Step 3: Fix the underlying issue

Use the cooldown productively. Address whatever caused the loss.

If it was unoriginal content: Stop reposting. Start transforming. Add captions, combine clips, add voiceovers, edit. The bar for "transformative" in 2026 is higher than it was even a year ago. If you cannot tell what you changed from the original, neither can Meta's reviewer.

If it was engagement bait: Audit your captions. Remove any with "tag", "like if", "comment your", "share if". Replace with descriptive captions. Going forward, never use engagement bait. Even if a competitor seems to get away with it, your page is now flagged and any further use accelerates the next restriction.

If it was inconsistent posting: Get back on schedule. Daily posts for the next 60 days minimum. The 60k-minute threshold needs sustained activity to recover. See our posting times guide for timing strategy.

If it was copyrighted music: Switch to Meta's licensed audio library or original audio for all videos. Audit your existing Reels and consider taking down the ones with copyrighted music to clear flags from your account.

If it was a series of small violations: Review every flagged post. Remove or revise the worst ones. Going forward, when in doubt about a post, do not publish.

Step 4: Appeal or wait

After the cooldown period passes, Meta re-evaluates automatically. You usually do not need to do anything. The eligibility banner updates from "not eligible" to "eligible" when the system decides to flip you back.

If after a full cooldown you are still ineligible, you can file an appeal through Meta Business Suite. Be specific about what changed. Include before-and-after evidence. Vague appeals get auto-rejected.

Real appeals that work:

  • "I reviewed the unoriginal-content flag and stopped reposting. All posts in the last 60 days are original transformations. Please re-evaluate."
  • "I audited my captions and removed all engagement bait language. Examples of previous violations and current compliant posts attached."

Appeals that do not work:

  • "I do not understand why I lost eligibility, please restore it."
  • "My competitors do the same thing and they are still monetized."
  • "This is unfair."

What NOT to do

Several recovery tactics make things permanently worse.

Do not buy fake engagement. Bot views and bot follows are detected by Meta's anti-spam systems within days. You go from temporary ineligibility to permanent ban.

Do not create a new page to replace this one. If Meta determines the new page is operated by the same person to evade restrictions, both pages get banned.

Do not delete and recreate. A deleted page cannot be restored. The cooldown clock resets to zero on the new page.

Do not contact Meta support repeatedly. One support ticket is fine. Five tickets in a week marks your account as a problem case.

Do not appeal during the cooldown. It will be auto-rejected and the rejection may extend your cooldown.

Prevention going forward

After recovery, your page is on a watchlist for at least 6 months. One additional violation in that window triggers permanent loss.

To prevent re-loss:

Maintain consistent posting. Use a scheduler. Our guide on running 5+ Facebook pages without burning out covers the operational side.

Default to original transformations. Never repost without modification, even if you think the original creator would not mind.

Avoid engagement-bait language entirely. Even casual phrases like "what do you think?" can register as engagement bait on flagged accounts. Use descriptive captions instead.

Audit your back catalog. Old posts can still trigger new violations if Meta re-scans your library. Remove or revise risky historical content.

Watch your insights for early warnings. Sudden drops in reach, engagement, or video minutes often precede an eligibility hit. Address the underlying cause before Meta does.

When to give up

Sometimes recovery is not realistic. If your page has had multiple monetization losses, is on a permanent watchlist, or experienced a severe violation, you may spend months trying to recover something that is fundamentally broken.

The honest math: if your page earned under $200 per month before the loss, the time spent recovering may not be worth it. Starting fresh with a new page on a new account (different operating account, not just a renamed page) may be faster.

If your page earned over $1000 per month before the loss, recovery is almost always worth pursuing. Even months of cooldown beats starting from zero followers.

For somewhere in between, do the math against your other obligations. A recovery effort that takes 90 days of cooldown plus 30 days of consistent posting before re-eligibility is essentially a 4-month project with no guaranteed outcome. Make sure that fits your situation.
